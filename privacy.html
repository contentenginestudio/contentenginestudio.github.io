Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-20

This policy explains what information is handled by (a) this website and (b) Content Engine, the private app we use to publish videos to our own channels and read their statistics. "We", "us" and "the owner" mean Content Engine Studio and the person who runs it.

In short: this website has no accounts, cookies, analytics or forms. Content Engine is used only by us, on accounts we own. It handles our own videos and our own channel statistics. It does not collect personal information about viewers or the public, and we do not sell, rent or share platform data for advertising.

1. This website

This is a static website. It has no user accounts, no sign-up or contact forms, no cookies, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party scripts or fonts. We do not collect any information through it.

The company that hosts the site may keep standard server logs (such as IP address, browser type, the page requested and the time) to run and protect its service. Those logs are handled under the hosting provider's own privacy policy; we do not use them to identify visitors. If you email us, we receive your email address and message and use them only to reply.

2. Who can use Content Engine

Content Engine is a private tool, not a public product. There is no way to sign up for it. It is used only by the owner and team members the owner authorizes, and only with platform accounts that we own and control. It runs on the owner's own computer.

Because of this, the only "users" whose accounts the app connects to are our own channel accounts. When this policy talks about "your" data in the platform sections below, it means the data of those accounts.

3. What Content Engine accesses

Content Engine connects to each platform only through that platform's official API, after we sign in on the platform's own page and grant access. It requests only the permissions it needs for the features below.

YouTube (Google)

  • Upload videos to our channels and set their titles, descriptions, tags, privacy status, publish time and thumbnails.
  • Create and manage playlists on our channels and add our videos to them.
  • Read basic information about our channels and videos (such as channel ID, video IDs and processing status) to confirm uploads worked.
  • Read YouTube Analytics for our channels: aggregate numbers such as views, watch time, average view duration, likes and subscribers gained. These figures are totals and averages; they do not identify individual viewers.

OAuth scopes requested:

ScopeWhy
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.uploadUpload our videos.
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-sslSet thumbnails and metadata, manage playlists, captions and comments on our own videos, and read our channel and video details.
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonlyRead our channels' analytics.

TikTok (planned)

  • Post our videos to our own TikTok accounts, or upload them to our TikTok drafts to finish in the TikTok app, using the TikTok Content Posting API.
  • Read basic profile information of the connected account (such as its display name and avatar) to confirm which account is connected, and check the status of each post.

Facebook and Instagram (Meta, planned)

  • Publish our videos to Facebook Pages we manage and to our Instagram professional accounts linked to those Pages.
  • Read insights for those Pages, accounts and posts: aggregate numbers such as reach, plays and engagement.

What it does not access

Content Engine does not read, collect or store viewers' names, profiles, messages, comments, contacts or other personal information. It does not access any account that we do not own.

4. How we use it

We use this access only to publish our own videos and to understand how they perform, so we can decide what to make next. Content Engine compares the performance of our own video formats (for example, which style of opening keeps people watching longer) and shows us daily summaries.

We do not use platform data for advertising, ad targeting, profiling of people, credit or lending decisions, or to train AI models.

5. What is stored, and where

  • Sign-in tokens. The OAuth access and refresh tokens that platforms issue to Content Engine are stored only on the owner's computer, in local files that are excluded from source control. They are never shared, published or sent anywhere other than back to the platform that issued them.
  • Our videos. The video files, thumbnails and text we publish are our own content and are stored on the owner's computer.
  • Performance statistics. Statistics about our own videos (such as views and watch time over time) are stored in a local database on the owner's computer.

Content Engine has no server of its own and does not upload this data to any cloud database.

6. Sharing

We do not sell, rent or trade any data, and we do not transfer data received from platform APIs to third parties, except in these limited cases that serve the owner directly:

  • The platforms themselves. Videos and their details are sent to YouTube, TikTok or Meta when we publish them.
  • Our private team chat. Content Engine posts a short daily summary of our own videos' numbers (for example, total views and our best and worst video) to a private Discord channel that only our team can read.
  • Our writing assistant. To help draft new video ideas, Content Engine may include short, aggregate notes about our own formats (for example, "question-style openings averaged more views on this channel") in requests to the AI text service we use for drafting (currently Anthropic's Claude API). These notes contain no viewer information, no account credentials and no raw analytics exports. We use that service under business terms that do not permit it to train its models on our requests.
  • The law. If we are legally required to disclose information, we will disclose only what is required.

7. YouTube API Services

Content Engine uses YouTube API Services. By using YouTube features through Content Engine, users agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Content Engine does not allow third parties to serve content or advertisements through it, and it does not place or read cookies or similar technology on anyone's device (see section 12). How we access, use, store and share YouTube data is described in sections 3 to 6 above.

8. Google API Services User Data Policy

Content Engine's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular, we use Google user data only to provide and improve Content Engine's features for its owner, as described above; we do not transfer it except as described in section 6; we do not use it for advertising; we do not let humans read it except our own team for our own channels, for security, or where required by law; and we do not use it to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models.

9. Retention and deletion

  • Tokens are kept only while we actively use the connection. Disconnecting an account in Content Engine revokes its token with the platform and deletes the local token file.
  • Performance statistics are kept as long as they are useful for comparing our videos over time. For YouTube data, Content Engine checks at least every 30 days that it is still authorized; any other stored YouTube data is refreshed or deleted within 30 days.
  • After access is revoked: if we disconnect an account from within Content Engine, we delete the data obtained through that platform's API within 7 days. If access is revoked from the platform's own settings page (for example, Google's security settings), we delete the related data within 30 days.
  • Our own videos are kept as long as we want to keep them; they are our content, not platform data.

Deleting data from Content Engine does not delete videos or data stored by YouTube, TikTok or Meta; those are managed in each platform's own settings.

10. How to revoke access

The account holder can remove Content Engine's access at any time, in addition to disconnecting it inside the app:

  • Google / YouTube: open the Google security settings page at security.google.com/settings/security/permissions (also reachable at myaccount.google.com/permissions), choose Content Engine and remove its access.
  • TikTok: in the TikTok app, go to Profile, then Settings and privacy, then Security & permissions, then Apps and services permissions, choose Content Engine and remove access.
  • Facebook: go to Settings, Business integrations, select Content Engine and remove it.
  • Instagram: Instagram professional accounts are connected through a linked Facebook Page, so removing Content Engine from Facebook's Business integrations removes its Instagram access too. You can also check Instagram's settings under Website permissions, Apps and websites.

11. Data deletion requests

To ask us to delete any data Content Engine holds about an account, email awesomeron34@gmail.com with the subject "Data deletion" and the account or channel name. We will delete it as soon as possible and within 7 days, and confirm by email. Because Content Engine only connects to our own accounts, in practice this concerns our own channels' data.

12. Cookies and device storage

This website does not use cookies, local storage or tracking pixels. Content Engine does not place cookies or similar technology on anyone's device. When we sign in to a platform to connect an account, that sign-in happens on the platform's own web page, which may use its own cookies under that platform's privacy policy.

13. Children's privacy

This website and Content Engine are not directed to children and do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. If you believe a child has sent us personal information by email, contact us and we will delete it.

14. Security

Tokens and statistics stay on the owner's computer, which is protected by a login password and disk encryption. Connections to platform APIs use HTTPS. No system is perfectly secure, but we limit what we store to what Content Engine needs.

15. Changes to this policy

If we change how Content Engine handles data, we will update this page and its effective date before the change takes effect. If a change would let Content Engine use data in a new way, we will ask for any platform permission it requires first.

16. Contact

Questions or complaints about this policy or our privacy practices: awesomeron34@gmail.com.
